How our apps are made, and why we tell you

17 November 2026

Anonaconda Code Lab is one person making small, free Android and web apps that keep everything on your phone. This post explains exactly how those apps are made, including the part some people won’t like: the code is written by an AI.

We think you deserve to know who, and what, made the software you trust with your photos, documents and heart data. So here it is, all in one place.

Why these apps

Every app here exists because I needed it myself, and I use at least one or two of them every day. That’s why the list looks a little odd: heart-rate and breathing apps next to tools for receipts and photos. They’re simply tools my daily life requires.

  • I use Rounds on every cardio day, which is four days a week. I like to mix it up a bit, which is why I added so many training modes to the app. My mornings always start with Still Point to check my HRV prior to training, though I admit, being a cycling addict, I don’t always abide by its recommendations!
  • I’ve always been interested in heart health and one thing I really longed for was an app that didn’t just show my ECG data from my Polar H10, but also provided an easy-to-understand summary of what each metric means in plain language. I also wanted to see how each session compares with previous sessions of the same type. It’s for these reasons that Lead One ECG was born.
  • The privacy apps came from everyday moments. The first time I saw how much a single photo gave away, I realised every picture I’d ever shared carried the exact spot where it was taken; now nothing I post goes anywhere without passing through Unmarked first. I made Redact because blacking things out in a normal photo editor doesn’t always hide them, and I wanted to be sure. I use Remark most often to mark up receipts, and photos before I send them to friends. And when I’m out and about with only my mobile, I scan with Flatbed, because I don’t want sensitive documents sitting on someone else’s server.
  • They also work as a team. A screenshot for a friend might go through Redact to cover my account number, then Remark to add the arrows. A photo of a receipt goes into Remark to note who paid for what, while the paper copy I need to keep becomes a clean PDF in Flatbed. And whichever app I finish in, the copy I share has had its hidden data removed by the same cleaner that powers Unmarked.

More generally, I’m deeply concerned about invasions of privacy by corporations for profit and manipulation, and by governments for surveillance and control. Unlike any other period in history, your pocket holds the story of your life. Every search, every message, every heart-rate spike, and every late-night thought is a piece of who you are. For years, the tech industry told us that for apps to be smart, useful, and fast, this data had to travel to distant servers in the cloud. That simply isn’t true: every app here does all its work on your phone. It is time to demand a new standard. What happens on your phone must stay on your phone.

How the apps are made

I decide what every app does, and Claude, Anthropic’s AI, writes the code. Every version then goes through the same steps before you see it:

  1. I design it. What the app is for, what goes in, and, just as important, what stays out: no account, no internet access, no ads.
  2. Claude writes the code. Since October 2026, every change in the public history names Claude as its author.
  3. Automated tests run on every change. Each app has its own tests, such as “does a cover really replace the pixels”, and every Android release is checked to make sure it has no internet permission.
  4. A security review before launch. Every app is reviewed for anything that could leak data before it goes public.
  5. CodeRabbit reviews every update. It’s an independent AI code reviewer from a different company. Every comment it makes is answered in public, fixed or explained.
  6. I test it on real phones: a Pixel 7a with GrapheneOS, a Pixel 6 Pro with stock Android, and a Poco X3 Pro with Evolution X. Nothing is released until it works on all three.
  7. Only I can approve the signed release. The key that signs the Android apps sits behind a step that needs my approval, every time.

Although I’ve dabbled in coding, I’m not a professional programmer, and I won’t pretend to be. But the decisions, the testing and the responsibility are mine. If an app breaks, I’m usually the first to notice, because I use it.

Why we say so openly

Because hiding it would be worse, and because you’d find out anyway: it’s in every commit.

AI-written code worries people, and some of the worries are fair:

  • Quality. Much AI code is rushed, untested and abandoned. That’s why every step above exists, and why all of it happens in public.
  • Where it came from. AI models learned from open-source code written by people. We give back the only way we can: every app is free and open source under the GPL, so anyone can read, change and build on it.
  • Developers’ work. Some developers feel AI is cheapening a craft they spent years mastering, and I don’t dismiss that. I’ve wanted to make apps for over a decade and even took courses in coding, but I never reached the level needed to build an app myself. AI hasn’t replaced a developer here: without it, these apps simply wouldn’t exist. And because they’re free and open source, every line is there for any developer to read, question, improve or reuse. And since our apps aren’t in any app store (not Google Play, where most paid apps are sold, nor even F-Droid), they mostly reach people who seek out open-source software, many of whom would never have bought a paid app anyway.

Some places won’t list AI-written apps at all, and some people will skip ours for that reason. That’s entirely their right, and we respect that. We’d rather lose a download than earn one by hiding how the app was made.

What we can’t promise

Being open means being honest about the limits too:

  • No app is perfect. Reviews and tests catch a lot, not everything. Redact can miss a face turned away; Unmarked can’t remove what you can see in a picture. Each app’s page says what it can’t do.
  • One person, not a company. There’s no support team, and updates come when I can make them. Every app keeps working offline, so nothing breaks if I’m away for a while.
  • The law around AI-written code is unsettled. Courts haven’t decided how copyright applies to it. Whatever they decide, the apps stay free for you to use and keep.
  • Health apps are for wellness, not medicine. Lead One ECG and Still Point show what your heart is doing; they don’t diagnose anything. Talk to a doctor about anything that worries you.
  • Not on Google Play. We publish directly instead, because we support Keep Android Open. It means installing takes an extra step or two. Our Why page explains why, and how.

Check us yourself

Don’t take our word for any of this. Everything is out in the open:

  • The code and its history: github.com/Anonaconda-CodeLab. Every change, every CodeRabbit review and every answer.
  • What each app may do: the “What the Android app is allowed to do” section on each app’s page at anonacondacodelab.com.
  • That your copy is genuine: each Android release lists its checksum and our signing certificate, and our Installer checks both for you.
